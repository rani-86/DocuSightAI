Spearman(degrade_level, legacy noise score) = 0.21 (p=1.7e-03); Spearman(degrade_level, legacy blur score) = -0.20 (p=2.3e-03).

**The legacy gates are largely uninformative on this data:**

* Legacy noise score is exactly 0.0 on 203/220 documents (max 3.0, gate 4). It is the median absolute residual after a 3x3 median filter; on a page that is mostly flat background more than half of all pixels have residual 0, so the median is 0 no matter how much noise there is in the minority of pixels. `denoise` therefore fired on 0/220 documents.
* Contrast (p95-p5) is below the gate of 150 on 187/220 documents (85%), because on a mostly white text page the 5th percentile is still white. CLAHE is effectively always on and carries no information.
* Blur (Laplacian variance) spans more than a decade across clean-looking pages (p10-p90 above), i.e. it varies with page content (text density, ruled tables) as much as with focus.

## 4. Is the gap within sampling noise?

Document-level (cluster) bootstrap, 5,000 resamples; whole documents are resampled because the fields of one page are correlated. 95% percentile intervals:

| Bucket | Docs | Field accuracy | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| light | 58 | 99.40% | 98.46% - 100.00% |
| medium | 94 | 95.32% | 91.92% - 98.15% |
| heavy | 68 | 98.61% | 97.47% - 99.54% |

Heavy - medium accuracy difference: **+3.29%**, bootstrap 95% CI [+0.24%, +6.81%], permutation-test p = 0.079.

Light - medium: +4.09%, CI [+1.14%, +7.57%], p = 0.043.

## 5. Is it a layout / document-type mix effect?

Layout variants differ a lot in difficulty (the borderless tables and 'value below label' layouts are harder). Expected accuracy for each bucket is computed from the *overall* accuracy of each (doc type, layout variant) stratum, weighted by that bucket's mix. If a bucket's observed accuracy is far below its expectation, mix does not explain it.

## Verdict

1. **The heavy-vs-medium gap is borderline, not clearly real and not clearly noise.** Heavy - medium = +3.29%; the bootstrap 95% CI [+0.24%, +6.81%] only just excludes 0, the permutation test gives p = 0.079. With three pairwise bucket comparisons (light/medium p = 0.043, light/heavy p = 0.287) nothing survives a Bonferroni correction (0.05/3 = 0.017). The medium bucket has 26 errors in 94 docs; only 13 docs contain them, so the effective sample is small.
2. **Layout mix does not explain it.** Given its mix, medium would be expected to have 17.9 errors but has 26; light and heavy have fewer than expected (2 vs 7.1, 6 vs 9.1).
3. **`degrade_level` is a weak predictor of failure.** Error rate by quintile is non-monotonic (peaks in quintiles 3-4, drops in 5) and Spearman(level, errors per document) = 0.11 (p = 0.11). 'Heavy' is not harder than 'medium' on this set. A plausible reason is that `degrade_level` scales only some of the randomly drawn artefacts (noise sigma, blur, rotation); phone-photo warp, lighting gradient, brightness shift and JPEG quality are drawn independently and are not recorded in the labels, so the bucket is a noisy proxy. I cannot say which hidden factor drives the medium errors without the true per-image parameters (Stage 3's controlled set addresses this).
4. **The gates cannot be the cause either way:** the noise gate never fired and the contrast gate almost always fired (section 2), so the preprocessing decision was nearly identical across buckets except for deblur (2% / 9% / 28% of light / medium / heavy), which fired on documents with a *higher* error rate (5.2% vs 2.2%) - consistent with deblur being triggered by hard pages rather than hurting them, but this analysis cannot separate the two (the ablation in Stage 6 can).
